# Functional traits as a tool for predicting coexistence in spatially heterogeneous environments
This repository contains data and analyses for my second and third chapters of my dissertation at Utah State University.

## Background
Spatial heterogeneity in the environment is an often undetectable and ignored confound in ecological field studies. However, heterogeneity in resources for plants, even on small scales, can have significant impacts on species coexistence and community composition. One way to measure this ubiquitous but unseen confounding variable is to measure functional traits, which can not only reflect the underlying spatial heterogeneity, but also influence species interactions. To understand how spatial heterogeneity influences plant communities, we need to connect functional traits to competitive outcomes between plants in spatially heterogeneous environments.

## Data collection
Data were collected June-July 2023 at the Sheep Experiment Station in Dubois, ID. I included six field sites: three around 5500' and three around 6000'. I collected the following annual species found along this elevation gradient: *Alyssum desertorum*, *Collinsia parviflora*, *Collomia linearis*, *Cordylanthus* sp., and *Polygonum aviculare*. 

Shrubs create patches of increased soil moisture and nutrients, and the distance to the center of a shrub was used as a proxy for distance to a "good patch" in these spatially heterogeneous plots. Two shrubs at each site were selected as "trait shrubs", around which I measured traits of individual plants and their distances to the center shrub (distance to a good patch). I didn't establish "competition shrubs," around which I'll measure neighbor density, growth, and fecundity of individual plants, in 2023.

I measured the following traits in 2023: height (cm), phenophase (categorical: 1=vegetation, 2=budding, 3=flowering, 4=senescence, 5=fruiting, 6=seed release), specific leaf area (mg/cm2), and average seed mass (mg). My predictor variables were distance to the center of the shrub (cm), aspect (north or south side of shrub), and elevation (low or high).
